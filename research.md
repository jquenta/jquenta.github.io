### Link invariants in discrete and continuous nilpotent group gauge theories

<div class="research-item">
  
  <div class="research-text" markdown="1">

  The first half of [this](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18237) paper investigates expectation values of links in untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) theories with nilpotent gauge groups. The motivation is the following: abelian TQFTs are *exactly* those that do not detect the Borromean rings, and we wanted to generalize this statement. Indeed, we prove that untwisted DW theories with $n$-step nilpotent gauge groups are exactly those that detect Brunnian links with up to $n+1$ components, but no more than that. 

  The second half instead deals with Chern-Simons (CS) theories with nilpotent Lie algebras. These are quite exotic since the gauge group is necessarily non-compact, so one finds a continuous spectrum of line operators, labeled by (generically) infinite-dimensional representations. Following previous results in the literature, we propose a correspondence between nilpotent DW and CS theories that derive from the same "Lie ring". Namely, expectation values of line operators take a similar form in both sides (but the theories, we argue, are not equivalent!).

  *Joint work with Pavel Putrov and Thomas Nicosanti.*

### Topological invariants of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Chern-Simons theory from discrete gauge theories

<div class="research-item">
  
  <div class="research-text" markdown="1">

  In [this](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11704) paper, we argue that (2+1)d twisted Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) theories with gauge group $\mathrm{SL}(2,\F_q)$ encode many of the same topological invariants as the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Chern-Simons (CS) theory at large levels. Namely, the former know about the values of the CS functional on flat $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ connections, as well as their associated leading powers in $k$ in the asymptotic expansion of the CS partition function. Our claims are supported by explicit computations of the DW partition function on a variety of (non-hyperbolic) 3-manifolds, which are possible after determining the modular data of the discrete gauge theories. We also provide some ideas for a proof when the 3-manifold is spherical, but otherwise, the mathematics underlying these relations remain a mystery!

  *Joint work with Pavel Putrov and Thomas Nicosanti.*
